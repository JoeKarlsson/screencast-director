# Renderer

- `Scene` draws a frame with Core Image + CoreText, `Composition` builds the AVComposition (cuts and speed as edits, muting and fades in an audio mix) with a custom compositor. The same composition feeds the preview player and the export.
- The camera eases asymmetrically (`cameraAt(lead:tail:)`: zooms start 0.3 s early with a 1.6x ease-out tail), with motion blur during moves; word timings snap to loudness at load (`WordTiming`).
- Motion (`Motion.swift`): `style.motion` picks a `MotionStyle`; the camera and layouts move through `EasedSteps` (explicit eased moves; scale in log space about the zoom's fixed point; `cameraSteps` times each zoom move by `cameraMoveFactor`, the square root of its distance, and ends every move in at the same frame; a cropped screen's rest pans park before each click) except in `classic`, which keeps the windowed `cameraAt`/`layoutAt`. Overlays get an `OverlayPose` from `Motion.pose`; `Scene.animated` draws a posed overlay, blurring it on its own layer when needed.
- `Polish.swift` (`director review`'s POLISH notes) walks `Scene.cameraState` frame by frame for clicks during camera moves, speed past 1.5x a standard move in the style, stutters and reversals, plus plan-level reading time, transitions, sound density and overlays entering together. Judgment calls, so they never fail `validate`.
- Crossfades (`segments[i].transition: "crossfade"`) keep the video's length: a second screen track (`handleTrack` in Composition.swift) carries the next clip's source just before the cut and the previous clip's just after, and `Scene.render` dissolves toward it (`crossfadeMix`). Like every track it's padded to the instruction's end.
- Every composition track must end exactly at the instruction's end. A camera track a few milliseconds long (scaled-range rounding) makes AVPlayer render nothing, black and silent, while `AVAssetImageGenerator` and export still work, so `director still` can't catch it. `buildComposition` trims to the frame count. Test playback with an `AVPlayerItemVideoOutput`, not just stills.
- An `AVAssetTrack` does not keep its `AVURLAsset` alive. Inserting from a track whose asset was freed fails with the unhelpful -11800/-12780; keep the asset in scope (see `camAsset` in Composition.swift).
- `annotations` rectangles are screen fractions mapped through `screenMap`, so they follow zooms; `blur` ones are applied to the frame in `privacyBlurs` before overlays.
- Animations (`Animation.swift`, `director/Animate.swift`, `Resources/animations/`): HTML/JS templates rendered by `director animate` with an offscreen WKWebView (init(config) once, seek(t) per frame at 12 fps, snapshots into an H.264 clip in `.animations/`, named by a hash of the data, cues, length and size). Motion blur averages up to 10 snapshots across a quarter of each frame, as many as the movement needs (`motionProbe` compares element boxes at the shutter's two ends; `Animation.subframeCount`), so templates must draw from `t` alone. Erase a dashed stroke with a stroke-dashoffset from 2 down to 1, never 0 to -1: macOS 26's WebKit draws a negative offset back in (the burst flickered there). After a render, `Animation.pops` compares each frame's tile colors (`popTiles`) with its neighbors and `director animate`/`animate-test` print a WARNING for anything on screen for one frame only; `animate-test.sh` fails on one. Flourishes live in engine.js (`land`, `burstMark`, `drawIcons`, `revealHeading`, and packets queued by `drawPath` and drawn each seek); the storyboard uses them all and frames the board with a camera that sums one spring per new node (`framing`, scale summed in log space so a pull-back zooms at a steady rate), starting 0.35 s before each lands. Nodes float, pop on a packet's arrival (`arrivalPulse`) and dip while a newer node lands; lines run edge to edge (`edgePoint`, node sizes measured at build). In portrait an animation's split eases in from its first frame and out to its last over at least `Scene.animationSplitSeconds` (cinematic ease), not centered on the edge. `land` sets `--lift` (1 in the air, 0 settled), which the paper theme's card shadows read. Every rendered frame also runs `overlapProbe` (Animate.swift) over the `#cam > .abs` pieces, and `director animate`/`animate-test` print a WARNING per overlapping pair or piece in the caption band; animate-test.sh checks it with two stickers on one spot. Every sixth frame `textProbe` lists each run of text (named by its `.abs` piece) with its color, and `TextContrast.rows` reads what's behind it from the frame (`TextContrast.backdrop`: the commonest color in its box, leaving out pixels of the text's own color, since a bold numeral can fill its box; a ring outside the box misses tight chip padding at small sizes) for WCAG contrast; held for two probes it's a WARNING, as is overlay text whose backdrop is see-through. animate-test.sh fails on one in a bundled template and checks the overlay hub's bare heading is caught. The lottie template plays `config.lottie` (the JSON of `data.lottie`, found like a template in `lottie/` of the video, personal or built-in folder, frozen into the video at render, and hashed into the clip's name) with lottie-web (`vendor/lottie_svg.min.js`, MIT, 5.12.2) via `goToAndStop` each seek; `pinAnimations` pauses every web animation at t, so templates may use CSS keyframes or `element.animate()`. The zine theme's lines are drawn by p5.brush (`vendor/brush.js`, MIT, standalone 3.0.0) on a WebGL canvas above the SVG lines (`drawBrushLines`): drawPath hides the SVG path and queues it. Its `brush.clear()` leaves transparent white, which shows as white, so the engine clears the canvas itself; the canvas is twice the clip's resolution, with points in pixels rather than `brush.scale`, which would enlarge the grain into blocks. WebKit stays out of DirectorCore and the app process; the app runs the bundled `director animate` when a clip is missing (`renderAnimationsIfNeeded`) and won't export until it exists. An `overlay` animation renders on a transparent page (`html.overlay` hides the paper, grain, vignette and marks; the WKWebView has `drawsBackground` off, so snapshots keep alpha and `draw` clears the buffer first) to HEVC with alpha in a .mov at `plan.animationOutput(a)` (the whole frame, even in portrait, which it doesn't split), and joins as an `overlay` insert drawn after full-frame B-roll and before frame effects; it covers nothing (`fullFrameCovers`, `cardCovers`, cuts, flashes and Polish skip it). Rendered clips join `inserts` as full-frame B-roll in `Scene`, after the plan's own, but get their own timeline row and `Selection.animation`. Each template has a `.json` manifest (`AnimationTemplate`: fields, cues, starting data) that the inspector's `AnimationEditor` and the ✦ Animate button are built from. `AnimationSources` finds templates and logos in the video's `animations/` folder, then the personal library, then the bundle; the renderer injects the template's script and passes logos as file URLs. Templates freeze into the video on first use, and `clipPath` hashes the frozen copy, so library changes never touch a finished video. The page code must await WebKit (callAsyncJavaScript, takeSnapshot), never spin the run loop: the CLI's top level is async, so spinning blocks the main queue WebKit calls back on. The `/animate` skill covers adding one.
- Animation template scripts run in JavaScriptCore in `Checks.swift` (`animationScripts`: the real engine.js and templates against a stand-in page), so template logic like which logos fill the hub's field is checked without WebKit.
