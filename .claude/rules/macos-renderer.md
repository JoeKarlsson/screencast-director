# Renderer

- `Scene` draws a frame with Core Image + CoreText, `Composition` builds the AVComposition (cuts and speed as edits, muting and fades in an audio mix) with a custom compositor. The same composition feeds the preview player and the export.
- The camera eases asymmetrically (`cameraAt(lead:tail:)`: zooms start 0.3 s early with a 1.6x ease-out tail), with motion blur during moves; word timings snap to loudness at load (`WordTiming`).
- Motion (`Motion.swift`): `style.motion` picks a `MotionStyle`; the camera and layouts move through `EasedSteps` (explicit eased moves; scale in log space about the zoom's fixed point; `cameraSteps` times each zoom move by `cameraMoveFactor`, the square root of its distance, and ends every move in at the same frame; a cropped screen's rest pans park before each click) except in `classic`, which keeps the windowed `cameraAt`/`layoutAt`. Overlays get an `OverlayPose` from `Motion.pose`; `Scene.animated` draws a posed overlay, blurring it on its own layer when needed.
- `Polish.swift` (`director review`'s POLISH notes) walks `Scene.cameraState` frame by frame for clicks during camera moves, speed past 1.5x a standard move in the style, stutters and reversals, plus plan-level reading time, transitions, sound density and overlays entering together. Judgment calls, so they never fail `validate`.
- Crossfades (`segments[i].transition: "crossfade"`) keep the video's length: a second screen track (`handleTrack` in Composition.swift) carries the next clip's source just before the cut and the previous clip's just after, and `Scene.render` dissolves toward it (`crossfadeMix`). Like every track it's padded to the instruction's end.
- Every composition track must end exactly at the instruction's end. A camera track a few milliseconds long (scaled-range rounding) makes AVPlayer render nothing, black and silent, while `AVAssetImageGenerator` and export still work, so `director still` can't catch it. `buildComposition` trims to the frame count. Test playback with an `AVPlayerItemVideoOutput`, not just stills.
- An `AVAssetTrack` does not keep its `AVURLAsset` alive. Inserting from a track whose asset was freed fails with the unhelpful -11800/-12780; keep the asset in scope (see `camAsset` in Composition.swift).
- `annotations` rectangles are screen fractions mapped through `screenMap`, so they follow zooms; `blur` ones are applied to the frame in `privacyBlurs` before overlays.
- Animations (`Animation.swift`, `director/Animate.swift`, `Resources/animations/`): HTML/JS templates rendered by `director animate` with an offscreen WKWebView (init(config) once, seek(t) per frame at 12 fps, snapshots into an H.264 clip in `.animations/`, named by a hash of the data, cues, length and size). Motion blur averages up to 10 snapshots across a quarter of each frame, as many as the movement needs (`motionProbe` compares element boxes at the shutter's two ends; `Animation.subframeCount`), so templates must draw from `t` alone. Erase a dashed stroke with a stroke-dashoffset from 2 down to 1, never 0 to -1: macOS 26's WebKit draws a negative offset back in (the burst flickered there). After a render, `Animation.pops` compares each frame's tile colors (`popTiles`) with its neighbors and `director animate`/`animate-test` print a WARNING for anything on screen for one frame only; `animate-test.sh` fails on one. Flourishes live in engine.js (`land`, `burstMark`, `drawIcons`, `revealHeading`, and packets queued by `drawPath` and drawn each seek); the storyboard uses them all and frames the board with a camera that sums one spring per new node (`framing`, scale summed in log space so a pull-back zooms at a steady rate), starting 0.35 s before each lands. Nodes float, pop on a packet's arrival (`arrivalPulse`) and dip while a newer node lands; lines run edge to edge (`edgePoint`, node sizes measured at build). In portrait an animation's split eases in from its first frame and out to its last over at least `Scene.animationSplitSeconds` (cinematic ease), not centered on the edge. `land` sets `--lift` (1 in the air, 0 settled), which the paper theme's card shadows read. Every rendered frame also runs `overlapProbe` (Animate.swift) over the `#cam > .abs` pieces, and `director animate`/`animate-test` print a WARNING per overlapping pair, piece in the captions' band (`Plan.captionBand`: landscape's bottom 150/1080, a vertical overlay's 62-78%, or around a dragged `captions.y`; none for a vertical card animation) or, in an overlay, piece cut off by the frame's edge (`OVERLAY` in engine.js also stops the storyboard reframing); animate-test.sh checks it with two stickers on one spot. Each frame also runs `markProbe` (Animate.swift): a path of a sticker's `.mark` that has started drawing and crosses a run of the sticker's text (other than its `.marked` word) is a WARNING; the note lays its lines out clear of its mark's box (`markShape`), and animate-test.sh checks both. Every sixth frame `textProbe` lists each run of text (named by its `.abs` piece) with its color, and `TextContrast.rows` reads what's behind it from the frame (`TextContrast.backdrop`: the commonest color in its box, leaving out pixels of the text's own color, since a bold numeral can fill its box; a ring outside the box misses tight chip padding at small sizes) for WCAG contrast; held for two probes it's a WARNING, as is overlay text whose backdrop is see-through. animate-test.sh fails on one in a bundled template and checks the overlay hub's bare heading is caught. The lottie template plays `config.lottie` (the JSON of `data.lottie`, found like a template in `lottie/` of the video, personal or built-in folder, frozen into the video at render, and hashed into the clip's name) with lottie-web (`vendor/lottie_svg.min.js`, MIT, 5.12.2) via `goToAndStop` each seek, looping unless `data.loop` or the file's top-level `"loop"` is false, and boils through `#boil-1..3` six times a second. The built-in Lotties are generated by `macos/make-lotties.py` (edit it and rerun, never the JSON); `AnimationSources.lotties()` lists every folder's (the app's picker, `director lottie list`, `meta.d` as the description) and `addLottie` imports one; `pinAnimations` pauses every web animation at t, so templates may use CSS keyframes or `element.animate()`. Themes: paper and terminal are built into the page; any other `look.animationTheme` is a folder (`ThemeSources`: the video's `animations/themes/<name>/`, then the support folder's `Animations/themes/<name>/`, where packs install it) with `theme.json` {title, css, scripts, marks, coverMarks}. `Animate` evaluates its scripts after the template and passes `themeCSS` and `marks` in the config; a script registers `THEMES[name]` hooks (setup, stroke, curve, draw, highlight, beforePackets, tick; see engine.js), `aboveLines` stacks a theme's canvas above the lines and under the packets. A theme freezes into the video like a template (`renderMissing`, `buildComposition`) and its digest joins the clip's name; a missing one fails `validate` and the render, and native drawing falls back to the app's marks. Marks (`MarkSet`, `Marks.swift`): name → SVG path in a `0 0 w h` viewBox, the app's drawn by `macos/make-marks.py` into MarksBuiltIn.swift (edit the script, never the Swift), a theme's marks.json over them; the page gets them as `SCRIBBLES` (`burstDense` is the burst, for frozen templates) and `Scene.marks` draws big chapters with them; covers get them only from a theme with `coverMarks`. `ChecksFiles`' brand check keeps one person's or company's look out of Sources/ and Resources/: it goes in a pack (`Packs.swift`, `director pack`). WebKit stays out of DirectorCore and the app process; the app runs the bundled `director animate` when a clip is missing (`renderAnimationsIfNeeded`) and won't export until it exists. While paused inside the selected animation, the app's `LiveAnimationPreview` keeps a `director animate-preview` running (a page stays loaded until the animation, look or size changes; one PNG per JSON request on stdin, sent the plan in memory) and puts each frame in `LiveFrames`, which the preview's Scene draws in place of that animation's clip through the same sticker/overlay/full-frame code; the preview Scene keeps an insert for every animation, rendered or not, and exports never get a `LiveFrames`. A new frame redraws by giving the player item a copy of its video composition (a seek right after a rebuild read time 0 and moved the playhead). An `overlay` animation renders on a transparent page (`html.overlay` hides the paper, grain, vignette and marks; the WKWebView has `drawsBackground` off, so snapshots keep alpha and `draw` clears the buffer first) to HEVC with alpha in a .mov at `plan.animationOutput(a)` (the whole frame, even in portrait, which it doesn't split), and joins as an `overlay` insert drawn after full-frame B-roll and before frame effects; it covers nothing (`fullFrameCovers`, `cardCovers`, cuts, flashes and Polish skip it). An animation with `sticker` ({x, y, w, rotation}) is a pop-up over the video drawn by the meme-sticker code (`Scene.sticker`: white border, tilt, the `stick` pop in and out): `animationOutput(a)` is the sticker's width, square, and after `init` the renderer cuts the clip to `stickerHeight()` (the template's `#sticker` card; the page itself isn't resized, which zoomed the snapshots), so the border hugs the card. The page gets `html.sticker-page` (not `.sticker`: that's the storyboard's node class, and on `<html>` it shrank the page into a 120 px box). `Scene.stickerRect` moves any sticker clear of `Plan.captionShares` (above, else below). `Animation.floats` (overlay or sticker) keeps both from covering or splitting the frame. The note template is the usual sticker. Rendered clips join `inserts` as full-frame B-roll in `Scene`, after the plan's own, but get their own timeline row and `Selection.animation`. Each template has a `.json` manifest (`AnimationTemplate`: fields, cues, starting data) that the inspector's `AnimationEditor` and the ✦ Animate button are built from. `AnimationSources` finds templates and logos in the video's `animations/` folder, then the personal library, then the bundle; the renderer injects the template's script and passes logos and fonts as `sd-asset://` URLs served from a list made per render (`Animate.Assets`). A template can come with a video someone sent, so an animation page (`Page.offline`) reads only `Resources/animations/`, has no network (a WKContentRuleList) and never navigates; animate-test.sh checks a project template can load its logo and no other local file. Folders the engine writes or deletes in go through `PathSafety.ownedFolder`, which refuses a symlink. Templates freeze into the video on first use, and `clipPath` hashes the frozen copy, so library changes never touch a finished video. The page code must await WebKit (callAsyncJavaScript, takeSnapshot), never spin the run loop: the CLI's top level is async, so spinning blocks the main queue WebKit calls back on. The `/animate` skill covers adding one.
- Animation template scripts run in JavaScriptCore in `Checks.swift` (`animationScripts`: the real engine.js and templates against a stand-in page), so template logic like which logos fill the hub's field is checked without WebKit.
