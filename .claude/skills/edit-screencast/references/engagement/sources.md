# Sources

Every rule in this folder comes from third-party sources read in November 2026, plus measurements of 31 hit videos by other creators. None of it comes from Joe's own videos or scripts.

Tiers, as the research rated them:

- **A:** a platform's own docs, blog or staff on the record (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, LinkedIn, TED).
- **B:** a study or a data set.
- **C:** a practitioner strategist. Opinion unless it shows data.
- **D:** editing craft or general reference. Opinion.

A rule backed only by C or D says so where it's stated.

## Measured videos

Each was downloaded at low resolution for measurement only and deleted afterwards. Measured: hard cuts (ffmpeg scene score over 0.3), visible change (frames 2 s apart with at least 1% of pixels changed by more than 24 levels), and, from the captions, when the title is proved, re-hooks, asks and the ending. A moving face counts as change, so the "nothing new" numbers only apply to screen-led video.

## Citation spot-check

Ten ledger rows picked at random were fetched again. Eight matched word for word. Two were right in substance but cited the wrong details:

- The Paddy Galloway quote is from his "20 lessons" thread of 2024-09-05, not the 2025 thread first cited.
- The umbrellacreators cut-rate figures are "picture changes" across three channels, and 14.7 a minute is their corrected value.

## Studies and data (tier B) the rules lean on most

- Mayer's multimedia principles (meta-analyses): temporal contiguity, signals on their word, d=1.31; signaling d=0.70; on-screen text that repeats the narration d=0.10 against; labels next to the thing they name d=0.82.
- Guo, Kim and Rubin 2014, "How video production affects student engagement" (edX, 6.9 million sessions): median watch time is at most 6 min whatever the length; tutorials are watched 2 to 3 min at a time.
- Kim et al. 2014, "Understanding in-video dropouts and interaction peaks" (edX): 61% of interaction peaks fall on visual transitions; 23% are viewers going back to code that left the screen.
- Wistia: a closing summary cost viewers about 40 s early.
- JRIM (8,500 videos): a generic "like and subscribe" had no measurable effect on subscribes or comments.
- Hoyos (Creator Insider): one second trimmed from a Short's end took retention from 83% to 88%.
