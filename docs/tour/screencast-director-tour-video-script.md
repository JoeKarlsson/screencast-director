# Screencast Director: the tour

**Format:** Short-form, in-app tour (plays from Getting Started), 3 min
**Words:** 432 (about 3 min at 140 words a minute)
**Audience:** someone who just opened Screencast Director for the first time
**Title/thumbnail:** none (in-app). The first frame is the finished tour playing in the editor.
**Angle:** You talk through your demo once, and the app does the boring part of the edit.

---

*[Screen: the editor with the sample recording open: blue clips, orange 3x clips, dashed cuts. In the edit, put the finished tour under this line.]*

I recorded the video you're watching right now in this app. And I didn't edit it. Well, I edited it a little. The app did most of it.

*[Camera: Joe, full frame.]*

So this is Screencast Director. Not director like Spielberg. Then again, Spielberg yells "cut" a lot, and so does this app. So, kind of like Spielberg.

Because editing a screen recording is the worst part, right? You scrub through every "um," every dead pause, every time you lost your cursor.

*[Screen: point at the Record button and the menu bar icon. Don't press the shortcut: it would stop this recording.]*

From any app, press Control, Option, Command, R. It records your screen, your mic, and your camera if you want it. It also notes where you click and when you type, but never what you typed.

*[Screen: open the sample recording you made before this take. Its draft edit is already there.]*

When you stop, it transcribes you right on your Mac and drafts the edit, with one rule. I call it the talking rule. If you're talking, it plays in real time. If you're typing and not talking, it's fast-forward. And if nothing's happening at all, it's gone.

*[Skit: Joe: "Hey, so I spent like eleven seconds looking for my cursor." App voice, flat: "Gone."]*

It also zooms in where you click, and turns what you said into captions.

*[Screen: the timeline. Point at the Clips, Words, Zoom, Layout and Titles tracks.]*

Down here's the timeline. Blue plays in real time, orange is sped up, and the dashed boxes are cuts. Click a cut and hit Restore to put it back, in case it cut your best joke. It won't. Probably.

*[Screen: press C to split, press Z to add a zoom, then drag the focus box in the inspector.]*

If you've used Screen Studio, the keys match. Space plays, J, K and L shuttle, C splits. Z adds a zoom. Drag this box to aim it.

*[Screen: type "cut my retakes and zoom in when I mention the timeline" into the Ask box. The timeline redraws.]*

Or, and this is the part I actually built it for, you just ask. "Cut my retakes and zoom in when I mention the timeline." It reads the transcript and rewrites the edit (or tries to, anyway). Command Z undoes whatever the AI did, which makes it the shortcut I use most.

*[Screen: Settings, AI editing.]*

By default it uses your Claude Code login, so there's no key to paste. Or point it at OpenRouter, or a model on your own machine.

*[Screen: the Style tab. Pick a profile, then switch Shape to 9:16 Vertical.]*

In Style, a profile sets your look: the background, fonts, colors, your logo. For a vertical clip, set Shape to 9:16. It crops the screen and follows your clicks, so your code's still readable on a phone. That one's my favorite.

*[Screen: open the Export menu, then click Publish.]*

Export has presets for YouTube and Shorts. Publish writes your captions, chapters and a description. It even picks thumbnails from frames where you're smiling.

*[Camera: Joe, a big forced grin.]*

So smile at least once. Like this. *[Beat.]* That's a thumbnail now.

*[Camera: Joe, full frame, then the editor behind him.]*

Remember the talking rule? You talk, and the app handles the boring part of the edit. I made this video the same way.
